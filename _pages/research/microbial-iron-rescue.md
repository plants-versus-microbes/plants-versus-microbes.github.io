**Root-associated microbes can change how plants cope with drought and nutrient stress.**

## Overview

Some bacteria, including Streptomyces, can restore plant iron uptake under drought conditions. We investigate the traits that enable this rescue and the conditions under which plants benefit.

## Key questions

- Which microbes restore plant iron uptake during stress?
- Which microbial genes and mechanisms enable rescue?
- How do plant genotype, microbial genotype, and soil conditions shape the outcome?

## Approach

We isolate and characterize root-associated bacteria and test their effects in controlled drought and nutrient experiments. Physiological assays and molecular analyses connect microbial traits to changes in plant iron uptake and growth.

## Why it matters

This work aims to identify microbial traits that support plant nutrition under stress and explain why beneficial interactions succeed in some environments but fail in others.
