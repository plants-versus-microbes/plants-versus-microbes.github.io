**Protecting pollinators means understanding the diseases that affect their developing young.**

## Overview

Alfalfa leafcutting bees (_Megachile rotundata_) are important pollinators in alfalfa seed production. Chalkbrood, caused by the fungus _Ascosphaera aggregata_, kills developing larvae and can undermine the health and productivity of managed bee populations.

This research brings a fungal disease perspective to pollinator health. Our goal is to better understand chalkbrood and generate knowledge that can support healthy bees and reliable agricultural pollination.

## Key questions

- How does _Ascosphaera aggregata_ infect and develop within bee larvae?
- Which conditions favour infection and the persistence of the pathogen in nesting systems?
- How can reliable pathogen identification and disease assessment support monitoring and management?

## Approach

The project connects fungal disease biology with the practical challenges of managing alfalfa leafcutting bees. A central priority is distinguishing the presence of a fungus from confirmed disease, and linking observations of the pathogen with effects on developing larvae.

## Why it matters

Healthy leafcutting bee populations support alfalfa seed production and the agricultural systems that depend on it. A better understanding of chalkbrood can inform disease monitoring and help guide future approaches to pollinator protection.
