**Restoring threatened pines requires a better understanding of the pathogen that challenges them.**

## Overview

White pine blister rust, caused by Cronartium ribicola, threatens whitebark and limber pine restoration. We investigate alternate host breadth and variation in pathogen virulence.

## Key questions

- Which alternate hosts support pathogen development?
- How does pathogen virulence vary?
- Which molecular markers could support disease monitoring and resistance breeding?

## Approach

We combine field surveys with controlled inoculation experiments to examine host susceptibility and pathogen performance. Molecular analyses help characterize variation among pathogen populations.

## Why it matters

This work aims to support restoration and resistance breeding for whitebark and limber pine, alongside improved monitoring of white pine blister rust.
